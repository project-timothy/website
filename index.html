What Tim is

Tim is an intelligent back office for world missions: software that does the administrative work of a sending organization, for the missionary and for headquarters, and speaks with people in plain language. Not a person, and not a portal. A colleague who never sleeps and never forgets.

He keeps the books, receipts, reports, filings, and policies current on his own, and brings each person one short brief with the one decision for today.

A missionary's hours go to the field. The sender's hours go to sending more workers.

"Be the missionary's helper: carry the practical work faithfully, so the missionary is free to preach the good news, to pray, and to steward the money well."Tim's charge

Where the hours go today

One third of a career is the floor.

Nobody in missions measures time on task, so these are the anchors that exist: agency data, the retention studies, and the missionaries' own accounts.

40 to 70 hours

a month of administration on the field: receipts, currency, reports, forms, renewals, claims. A quarter to nearly half of a working month.

22% of a career

spent raising the budget on a four-years-on, one-off cycle. On itineration the administration is the job: 40 to 70 hours a month booking services, up to 260 for an independent family.

5.1% a year

leave the field, and 71% of that is preventable. Two thirds of returning missionaries name discomfort raising money; half name low support.

42%

of the world's people have no access to the gospel. 202 million of them belong to 2,085 people groups with no church, no missionary, and no known believer.

Sources: ReMAP I (World Evangelical Alliance, 453 agencies); the 2017 survey of 745 former missionaries (Sears, 2023); Wycliffe and agency itineration guides; the 2026 Gospel Access Summit. Hour ranges are agency data, three independent anchors.

Multiplication, not addition

Ministry scales. The office does not.

What changed

  • Genius-level intelligence is now on staff. Reading, drafting, checking, and explaining, at the cost of a utility, for every unit and every desk.
  • Routine work now runs as a system. Books, receipts, filings, renewals, and reminders run end to end every night, and a person is asked only when a decision is needed.
  • Put together, ministry scales and the office does not. The same small team at headquarters can carry ten units or four thousand.

The arithmetic

  • Addition. Every worker sent adds a worker's paperwork. Headquarters grows with the field, and the field waits on headquarters.
  • Multiplication. Every worker sent adds a worker, and nothing else. Tim carries the office, so a thousand new workers arrive without a thousand new back offices.
  • The measure. Hours given back to the field, deadlines never missed, and how fast a new unit is fully running.

Send people, not paperwork.

What Tim does

For the missionary, and for the people who send them.

For the missionary

  • Books and receipts. Every gift, expense, and reimbursement recorded and reconciled, in any currency.
  • Reports and forms. Quarterlies, requests, and renewals filled from the ledger. The missionary reviews and signs.
  • Deadlines, paperwork already gathered. Visas, ID papers, tax filings, health coverage enrollments and claims, weeks early.
  • Church communication. Itinerating missionaries first, supported next, no church inundated.
  • One brief. What changed, what is due, and the one thing to decide today.

For the sender

  • Compliance at four levels. Federal, state, host country, and local field, checked nightly against a calendar of obligations, evidence attached.
  • Policies that stay current. Tim reads rule changes and meeting decisions, drafts the policy update, and a person approves it.
  • A back office that does not grow with the count. Ten units or four thousand, the same nightly run.
  • A nightly audit of his own work, every finding written down for a person to read.

Tim proposes. A person decides. Money never moves without a human yes.

A memory for the field

Context that outlives the assignment.

A family carries years of relationships and trust in a country. The father has a stroke, the next family arrives, and nobody can tell them who does what. Traction is lost.

The record: pictures and the people in them

  • Faces and names first. A photo of every worker, partner, pastor, and national leader, with who they are, what they do, and how they connect, so an incoming missionary knows the people before the first handshake.
  • Then the story so far. Projects, churches, decisions, and hand-off notes, kept current by Tim, so a successor starts on day one.

Two rooms

  • The open room. Fields that can be spoken of. Searchable, readable by the leaders who need it, so anyone responsible can see who is who in any country in a minute.
  • The sealed room. Sensitive fields, held the way a government holds classified material: named readers only, every look logged, nothing leaves, nothing ever published by software, and the sending organization holds the key.
  • The rule. A person decides what is sensitive. Tim enforces it.
How it works

Two layers, one voice.

  • Running today. The engine under Tim runs a real company's back office: books, receipts, approvals, a sealed monthly close, and a nightly audit, unattended since summer 2026.
  • An engine underneath, Tim on top. The accounting engine does the arithmetic and never invents a number. Tim reads from it, explains, and answers when asked. Any capable model can sit in his seat; none of them touch the books.
  • Runs where you say. A five-dollar server for one unit, or one server a sending organization owns. A missionary's family finances stay with the missionary.
  • Careful by design. Whoever runs Tim owns every record. Nothing given to Tim trains anyone's model, sensitive locations live in a list the organization keeps, and the sealed room is never read by one.
  • Nothing is replaced. Tim reads from the systems you already have.
Open and shared

One tractor, a standard hitch, and every farm's own field

Timothy lives in public repositories at github.com/project-timothy and will stay public, so a missionary anywhere can run the whole thing, and any technically minded volunteer can help. The machines are public. The fields, every ledger row, every gift, every name, belong to whoever drives the tractor onto them, and no field is ever published.

How people can help

Iron sharpens iron. The best ideas win, whoever brings them.

Missionaries

The listening tour is step one. An hour describing a real month is the most valuable contribution there is.

Churches & volunteers

Hosting. A church with one technically minded member can run Timothy for the missionaries it sends.

Developers

A module, an adapter to a system we have not met yet, a translation, tests against the synthetic missionary family.

Sending organizations

A pilot: blank copies of the forms filed most often, thirty minutes with whoever answers finance questions, ten units for one quarter, and we measure the hours given back to the field.
